Chunking strategies for RAG systems

Chunking is the step where a clean document becomes retrieval units. Get it wrong and even a strong embedding model cannot recover: a chunk that cuts a definition in half will not match the query it should answer.

Fixed-size windows

The simplest approach is to cut text into windows of N characters or tokens with a sliding overlap. It is cheap, deterministic and surprisingly competitive when the source documents already have short, self-contained paragraphs.

The failure mode is structural: a window can start in the middle of a sentence and end in the middle of the next one, so the chunk no longer expresses a complete thought. Overlap reduces the damage but never removes it.

Sentence-aware chunking

A better baseline respects sentence boundaries and packs whole sentences into a chunk until the size budget is reached. Recall improves noticeably on technical content because each chunk still reads like prose a human would write.

Semantic chunking

Semantic approaches embed consecutive sentences and split where the embedding similarity drops, which means a topic change becomes a chunk boundary. It costs an extra embedding pass over the corpus and adds a tuning knob, but it keeps each chunk topically uniform.

Overlap guidance

For most prose, 10 to 20 percent overlap is enough. Larger overlap inflates the index, duplicates evidence across chunks and can make the retriever return the same passage several times, which hurts answer diversity.

How to evaluate

Build a small set of real questions with known source passages, then compare chunking strategies on recall@k before touching anything else. Chunking changes the distribution of what can be retrieved, and that dominates downstream answer quality far more than prompt wording does.
